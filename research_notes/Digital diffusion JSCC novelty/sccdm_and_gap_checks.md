# SC-CDM, arXiv:2510.26442, and gap checks for digital (M-QAM) diffusion-receiver image JSCC

Checked 2026-11-08. Full texts were fetched from arXiv HTML (curl, converted to plain text, then grepped), so the quotes below are verbatim apart from math, which shows as [MATH]. "Abstract only, unverified" marks anything not read in full text.

## Q1. SC-CDM (arXiv:2410.02121): does it quantise channel symbols to a finite constellation? What does the diffusion model do? How does it handle SNR?

### Takeaway
No, SC-CDM is not digital. "The modulation order is set to 4" refers only to the JPEG+LDPC+QAM baseline. SC-CDM's own channel input is a continuous, real-valued Swin-Transformer latent with no quantisation step. Its diffusion model is a DiffIR-style restoration module: a JSCC decoder first produces a distorted image, and the diffusion model then refines it. It does not generate the image from noise conditioned on the received symbols. A single model covers 0 to 15 dB, and no SNR conditioning is described.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "SC-CDM: Enhancing Quality of Image Semantic Communication with a Compact Diffusion Model", Kexin Zhang, Lixin Li, Wensheng Lin, Yuna Yan, Wenchi Cheng, Zhu Han. arXiv:2410.02121v1, 3 Oct 2024, accepted at an IEEE Globecom 2024 workshop. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- **The modulation order applies only to the baseline.** §IV-A: "We compare our proposal with classical separation-based source and semantic communication. The traditional communication model considers the well-established image codec JPEG. The channel noise or fading is then processed using a Low-Density ParityCheck code (LDPC) and Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM) scheme, denoted as "JPEG+LDPC+QAM". The modulation order is set to 4. The semantic communication model employs the classical algorithm DeepJSCC." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- §IV-B also ties QAM to the baseline only: "while LDPC codes and QAM modulation increase transmission robustness, they do not fully compensate for the quality degradation in the presence of errors." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- **The channel input is continuous, with only a power constraint.** §II-A: "the transmitted signal [MATH] must satisfy the average power constraint [MATH]"; "[MATH] is the noise added by the channel, and [MATH] is the matrix of the corruption, indicating the missing portions of the transmitted content. Therefore, the received content [MATH] is a noisy and masked version of the original transmitted content". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- §III-A: "The captured key semantic information is encoded into the latent space denoted as [MATH] by the state-of-the-art vision transformer-swin Transformer" (the abstract says the latent is in ℝ^{H/8×W/8×C}, with C = 32); "the loss function adopts the MSE"; "the semantic decoder follows an inverse architecture that is symmetric with the encoder." Section III never mentions QAM, a constellation, or quantisation. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- The paper names DeepJSCC-Q only as related work. §I: "DeepJSCC has been adapted for digital communication systems as DeepJSCC-Q [8]". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- **The diffusion model refines a decoded image.** §III-B: "Inspired by [16], we develop the semantic fine-tuning module on top of an image restoration latent diffusion model, the core of which is a compact diffusion model. This model comprises two main networks: the slim prior network ([MATH]) and the image reconstruction network ([MATH])." Training proceeds as follows: "we initially concat the original training set images [MATH] with their corresponding decoded distortion images [MATH]". At inference: "the PRs of the decoded image [MATH] captured by [MATH] ... are used to apply the forward diffusion process"; "Since the pre-trained model only adds details for recovery, the compact diffusion model can obtain stable visual results after several iterations. After that, [MATH] utilizes [MATH] to recover the decoded image"; "The diffusion model aims to predict the noise distribution of the conditioned vectors of the decoded image". The total number of timesteps is 4 (§IV-A: "the total timesteps [MATH] are set at 4"). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)
- **SNR handling.** §IV-A: "All methods consistently use the CIFAR-10 dataset and are selected to be trained with SNRs between 1 dB and 13 dB. Then, the tests are performed under Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) and Rayleigh channel conditions, both under different channel conditions with PSNR values in the set [0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15]." (The text says "PSNR values" here, but in context it means the SNR test points.) §IV-B: "For the SC-CDM, a single model covers a range of SNR from 0 dB to 15 dB." No SNR input, SNR embedding, or channel estimation is described anywhere in §II–§III. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)

### Inferences
- SC-CDM is an analog Swin-JSCC followed by a post-hoc diffusion restoration step, similar to DiffIR or the "JSCC then diffusion refine" approach of DIFFSC. It is not prior work for a digital channel input, and it is not prior work for a diffusion receiver that generates from noise conditioned on received symbols. The phrase "modulation order of 4" is a false lead.
- Its single model covers 0 to 15 dB. Given the phrase "trained with SNRs between 1 dB and 13 dB", it was most likely trained with random SNR and no conditioning, which would make it SNR-agnostic or blind. This is inferred, not stated.

### Gaps
- Reference [16], the base restoration latent-diffusion model, is cited in the text but its title was not checked in the reference list. It is very likely DiffIR (Xia et al., ICCV 2023). This is an inference.
- The paper does not state how the power constraint is enforced, and does not state whether the latent is mapped to complex symbols.

## Q2. arXiv:2510.26442: is 16-QAM used for the proposed scheme's channel input, with a diffusion receiver? Is it learned JSCC or separation?

### Takeaway
16-QAM is used for the proposed scheme. However, the scheme is a **separation-based digital system** with no learned JSCC and no quantisation to a constellation. It takes a pretrained Stable Diffusion v2.1 VAE latent, serialises it to 64-bit IEEE-754 floats, applies a rate-1/2 convolutional code, and maps the bits to 16-QAM. The receiver is a pretrained SD v2.1 that runs caption-conditioned DDIM latent inpainting. Received latent blocks are clamped as known sites, and only withheld blocks are generated. SNR is handled by assuming an accurate SNR estimate at the receiver, which sets the inpainting strength through a heuristic. Nothing is trained per SNR. It therefore does not anticipate learned digital JSCC with a diffusion receiver.

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "Diffusion-Aided Bandwidth-Efficient Semantic Communication with Adaptive Requests", Xuesong Wang, Xinyan Xie, Mo Li, Zhaoqian Liu. v1 submitted 30 Oct 2025, v4 on 26 Jan 2026. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26442)
- §II-A: "The [MATH] blocks are serialized and modulated into baseband symbols, given by [MATH] (6) where [MATH] serializes the selected latent blocks into a bitstream using IEEE 754-2008 standard (64 bits per coefficient), [MATH] is a rate- [MATH] convolutional code, [MATH] maps bits to symbols using 16-QAM". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §II-A: "In experiments, we vary the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to emulate different link qualities under the same receiver-side reconstruction procedure, while leaving noise-aware latent refinement [18] to future work. Finally, both [MATH] and [MATH] are power-normalized and are sent into the same additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §II-B: "We assume the block mask [MATH] and block size [MATH] are conveyed as reliable control metadata (hence [MATH] and [MATH] are known), and [MATH] has an accurate SNR estimate." The receiver then applies the inverse operations (demodulation, decoding, deserialisation). — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §II-B: "Two parameters regulate the decoder's trade-off between caption conditioning and reliance on received blocks: the guidance scale [MATH] and the inpainting strength [MATH]. We fix [MATH] in our experiments. At round [MATH], define [MATH] and set [MATH] by a simple heuristic, where [MATH] decreases with SNR and increases with [MATH]. The sampler [MATH] then runs [MATH] DDIM steps". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §III-A: "We use Stable Diffusion v2.1 as the generative backbone [MATH] and BLIP-2 as the receiver-side captioner"; "Each image is resized and encoded by the Stable Diffusion v2.1 VAE encoder to obtain a latent grid, which is then partitioned into blocks for transmission". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §III-B: "This is consistent with our heuristic that decreases the inpainting strength and therefore the number of denoising steps at higher SNR. The sampler relies more on the received latent evidence and performs fewer corrective diffusion updates". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)
- §II-C: "We do not model packet erasures and assume a reliable feedback channel". — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)

### Inferences
- Classification: digital separation-based. It uses a pretrained VAE (frozen source representation), float serialisation, a classical convolutional code, 16-QAM and AWGN. The diffusion model is a frozen pretrained inpainter that fills in withheld latent blocks. It is not a receiver trained on channel symbols. There is no end-to-end learning, no learned mapping to the constellation, and no training per SNR.
- This paper is prior art for "QAM-modulated link plus pretrained diffusion at the receiver" in a separation design. It is not prior art for learned JSCC whose channel symbols are quantised to M-QAM and fed to a diffusion receiver.

### Gaps
- Exact SNR grid, block sizes and heuristic constants are in Table I, but their values were lost as [MATH] in the text conversion and were not recovered.

## Q3. Targeted searches: does any paper quantise JSCC channel symbols to a finite constellation and also use a diffusion model to generate or reconstruct the image?

### Takeaway
No such paper was found after 6 web searches, 1 arXiv API query, and full-text checks of 5 candidates. The closest digital and generative work, **SemQAM (arXiv:2610.29643, Tsinghua, 21 Oct 2026)**, maps VQ indices to QAM symbols with a single model across SNRs. Its generator is a VQGAN/PatchGAN-style decoder with autoregressive context coding, not a diffusion model. It must be cited as the nearest digital generative JSCC prior. The diffusion-receiver JSCC papers checked (CDM-JSCC, RC-BFM, SC-CDM) all transmit continuous symbols. DSRDM uses "QAM plus diffusion" in an unrelated sense: it recovers bits from diffusion noise and does not do image JSCC.

### Cited Findings
**SemQAM, arXiv:2610.29643v1 (cs.CV, 21 Oct 2026). Hao Cao, Jingkai Ying, Yinhuan Huang, Zhijin Qin, Yuan Shen (Tsinghua). This is the nearest prior: digital and generative, but not diffusion.**
- Abstract: "We propose SemQAM, a joint semantic-channel coding and modulation scheme for generative image transmission converting source to modulated symbols, such as standard QAM symbols. ... SemQAM represents latent features with vector quantization (VQ), maps each VQ index to a QAM symbol, and updates the codeword corresponding to the detected symbol so that channel impairments directly affect codebook learning. Moreover, SemQAM uses the conditional expectation of received codewords to approximate the receiver reconstruction and reduce error propagation during autoregressive coding. SemQAM supports multiple SNRs and CBRs in a unified model". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)
- §3: "Across channel conditions and transmission rates, SemQAM shares the image transforms, hyperprior, and context networks while selecting the corresponding VQ codebooks for each ope[rating point]". This means one shared model with a separate codebook selected for each SNR/CBR. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)
- The decoder is generative through GAN and perceptual losses, not diffusion. §3: "We use the EF-LIC image objective [10] ... [MATH] uses pretrained VGG features [43, 54], and [MATH] is the generator-side PatchGAN loss following VQGAN [17]." The word "diffusion" appears 4 times in the converted text, and all 4 are reference titles of baseline codecs (OSCAR, CoD-Lite, DiT-IC, and the one-step diffusion codec OneDC). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)
- Its framing of related work matches ours. §2: "Previous JSCC methods directly transmit continuous-valued encoded features [9, 13, 26]. JSCM instead maps the encoded features to a finite set of digital constellation points." The learned baselines are "NTSCC, NTSCC+, MDJCM-A, and Quad-JSCC", and the separated baselines are "MS-ILLM, OSCAR, OneDC, DiT-IC, EF-LIC, and CoD-Lite ... paired with LDPC and QAM". Main results are on "the AWGN channel at 10 dB", with extra results at 16 and 24 dB. DiT-JSCC is not among its baselines (a grep for "2601.03112", "DiT-JSCC" and "Tan," found no hits). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)

**CDM-JSCC, arXiv:2409.02597v2. Pujing Yang, Guangyi Zhang, Yunlong Cai (ZJU). Diffusion receiver conditioned on symbols, but analog and trained per SNR.**
- Abstract: "These symbols are then used at the receiver as conditional information to guide a conditional diffusion decoder in image reconstruction." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02597v2)
- The quantisation is for the entropy model on the latent, not a constellation for the channel symbols: "we estimate the distribution of [MATH], i.e., the quantized version of [MATH], by utilizing hyperprior entropy models." QAM appears only in baselines: "BPG+LDPC: ... followed by quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM)". The digital version of their rate-adaptive JSCC is a separate work: "This work was further evolved in [7] into a digital version". [7] is "From analog to digital: Multi-order digital joint coding-modulation for semantic communication," arXiv:2406.05437. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02597v2)
- Receiver: "The receiver comprises a rate-adaptive JSCC decoder and a conditional diffusion decoder"; "The conditional diffusion decoder iteratively refines the output". SNR: "In all subsequent experiments, the model is trained on a single SNR value and tested on the same SNR." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02597v2)

**RC-BFM, arXiv:2607.24876v1 (27 Jul 2026, Globecom 2026). Fan Gao, Youzheng Wang, Zhijin Qin, Feifei Gao (Tsinghua). Flow-matching receiver, analog.**
- "Low-Latency Generative Semantic Communication via Channel-Realization Flow Matching". The abstract says diffusion receivers "rely on stochastic iterative de[coding]". The baselines are "BPG + 5G LDPC [20], discriminative receiver DeepJSCC [5], diffusion-based receiver DiffCom [7], and FM-based receiver LTT [9]". A grep of the full text for QAM, QPSK, constellation and quantiz* returns no match for the proposed scheme. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24876)

**DSRDM, arXiv:2605.27730v1 (26 May 2026). QAM and diffusion in an unrelated sense.**
- "DSRDM encodes digital signals by gradually adding Gaussian signals to images in the forward diffusion process"; "By grouping the original bits into complex constellation symbols with [MATH]-ary modulation, the original digital signal is converted into an approximate Gaussian signal, which is then injected into the forward diffusion process"; "the underlying goal is not image reconstruction"; the metric is BER with "16-QAM & 256-QAM". It is not image JSCC. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27730)

**Base digital JSCC with no diffusion: DeepJSCC-Q (Tung, Kurka, Jankowski, Gündüz, IEEE JSAIT 2022).** It is "an end-to-end optimized JSCC solution for wireless image transmission using a finite channel input alphabet". From search snippets and prior knowledge; full text not re-fetched in this pass. — [arXiv:2206.08100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08100)

### Inferences
- The specific combination is a learned JSCC encoder, channel symbols quantised to M-QAM (hard or soft), and a conditional diffusion/DiT receiver that generates the image from pure noise conditioned on the noisy received constellation symbols. After these checks, this combination appears open. SemQAM covers the digital and generative part with a GAN-style decoder. CDM-JSCC, DiT-JSCC and RC-BFM cover the diffusion or flow receiver with analog symbols. 2510.26442 covers QAM with pretrained diffusion inpainting, but as separation with no learning.
- SemQAM is from the same Tsinghua group (Zhijin Qin) as RC-BFM and is dated 21 Oct 2026. Diffusion- or flow-based follow-ups to it are a plausible near-term risk.

## Q4. Papers citing DiT-JSCC (arXiv:2601.03112) up to Nov 2026: does any add quantisation, digital modulation, or SNR adaptivity?

### Takeaway
The citation indices list very few citers. OpenAlex reports `cited_by_count` = 3, and Semantic Scholar's citations endpoint returned 1. None of them adds digital modulation or quantised channel symbols to a DiT-JSCC-style receiver. The one SNR-related citer, PODD, adds channel-state conditioning to a diffusion JSCC denoiser, and its modulation was not verified.
