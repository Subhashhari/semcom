# Quantised DiT-JSCC occupies a narrow, defensible gap

A digital DiT-JSCC is novel, but only in a tightly scoped form. The novel system is a learned JSCC whose channel symbols are snapped to a fixed M-QAM grid (for example 16-QAM through DeepJSCC-Q's soft-to-hard quantiser) and whose receiver is a conditional diffusion transformer that generates the image from pure noise, using the received constellation symbols only as a condition. Searches up to 8 November 2026 found nobody who has built it. Each of its neighbours is taken, though. **Every diffusion receiver that generates images in JSCC sends analog symbols**: DiT-JSCC, DiffJSCC, CDM-JSCC, LRISC, DiffCom, SGD-JSCC, OSD-JSCC and SC-CDM. **Every digital diffusion scheme uses diffusion for something else.** In SCDM, SSCDM, CD3M and DCDDM it cleans received symbols before a deterministic decoder. In PDSC and the adaptive-request scheme it generates images behind a classical channel code. **The one QAM-native generative image JSCC, SemQAM (21 Oct 2026), uses a GAN-trained VQ decoder, not diffusion.**

On SNR, no generative image JSCC found is trained and tested blind. SGD-JSCC, the closest, works without the true SNR by *estimating* it and feeding the estimate in. No paper compares one model for all SNRs against an envelope of per-SNR specialists. A paper can therefore defend two scoped claims. The first is architectural (QAM-constrained symbols plus a pure-noise DiT receiver). The second is empirical: specialists vs an SNR-told model vs a blind model, plus an explicit estimator arm. Broad "first digital diffusion" or "first QAM generative JSCC" claims are already false. The repo's own positioning needs correcting too: **DeepJSCC-Q's 2021 conference paper already trains one quantised, SNR-attention model over 0–10 dB**, so ADJSCC-Q is not new as an idea. The residual risk comes from index lag and from very recent competitors: SemQAM, STI-SemCom and OSD-JSCC all appeared in the last three weeks, and the Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar citation lists were never fully checked.

## Every diffusion generator in image JSCC still transmits analog symbols

The prototype's target, DiT-JSCC (Tan, Dai, Wang, Lu, Shao, Niu, W. Zhang, P. Zhang; arXiv:2601.03112, v2 18 Jun 2026), is analog by construction. Its encoder maps the image to "a vector of complex-valued channel input symbols". These are power-normalised and sent, and QAM appears only in the LDPC separation baselines ("5G LDPC … with 4-ary quadrature amplitude modulation (4QAM)"). It also states that "we train separate models for each SNR under both AWGN and Rayleigh fading channels" ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03112v2)). Its only adaptivity is over bandwidth: a training-free allocation inspired by Kolmogorov complexity ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03112)). The future-work fragments that could be extracted point to "deployment-oriented variants" that reduce foundation-model and DiT cost. They say nothing about digital transmission or SNR adaptivity ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03112v2)). The public repo is "a self-contained inference release" and states that "The paper trains separate checkpoints for each channel/SNR condition". Its demo SNR slider changes only the simulated channel ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semcomm/DiTJSCC/main/README.md)).

Follow-up work on DiT-JSCC is thin. OpenAlex lists **3 citers** ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W7165539330)), Semantic Scholar returned 1, and Pith lists 2 ([Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2601.03112)). None of them quantises the symbols or makes the model SNR-adaptive. DDM-SSCC is lossless separate source-channel coding. SoM-MTM cites DiT-JSCC only as background. PODD (IEEE TMC, Sep 2026) adds multi-scale CSI to a diffusion denoiser, but only its abstract was read, so its modulation is unknown.

The wider family looks the same once each paper's own system is separated from its baselines:

Two entries were false leads, and both are resolved. SC-CDM's "modulation order is set to 4" describes only its JPEG+LDPC+QAM baseline, and its own latent is continuous ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)). Pei et al. use QAM language, but Algorithm 4 reads "Transmit real-valued z_R through noisy wireless channel", and the paper has no quantiser ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06644)). The structural point matters for positioning. In this literature, "digital" enters only through separation baselines, so a DiT receiver conditioned on finite-constellation symbols has not been tested anywhere.

## Digital diffusion exists, but as a symbol cleaner, a token filler or a separated decoder

Digital diffusion semantic communication is a populated field. The novelty claim survives only because every member of it gives diffusion a different job from DiT-JSCC's. There are four families, and each has a clear boundary.

**Diffusion that denoises or corrects constellation symbols.** This branch is the main prior art to cite. SCDM (ICC 2025) runs a score-based SDE on received 64-QAM symbols of a JCM codec. It drops the drift term because it "would lead to the variance of the constellation points collapsing around the origin", and sets "The denoising steps N_SNR" from channel conditions. One SCDM covers −18 to 18 dB, on CIFAR-10 ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17876v1)). SSCDM (Huh, Kang, Choi; accepted Sep 2026) states, "We assume 16-QAM modulation for transmission, leading to a codebook size of 16". A Markov-enforced discrete diffusion model corrects detected indices before a JSCC decoder reconstructs FFHQ-256. The start step comes from the SNR, and the codec is trained at a fixed 20 dB ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22983)). SSCDM's related work names two earlier discrete correctors, CD3M (IEEE WCL 2026) and DCDDM (IEEE TWC 2025). It also observes that in SCDM "the diffusion module primarily serves as a noise suppressor rather than a semantics-aware component" ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22983)). CD3M and DCDDM are known only through that description, and the IEEE Xplore page for CD3M returned empty ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/5962382/11304577/11305152.pdf)). In all four, the diffusion model cleans symbols and a deterministic decoder makes the image. None of them generates from pure noise.

**Token and bit separation with generative receivers.** Here the receiver is generative, but the link is classical. TokCom groups VQGAN tokens into packets with a "rate-1/2 convolutional encoding with cyclic redundancy check" and 16-QAM. Failed packets become "[MASK]" for MaskGIT ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12096)). Text-Guided TokCom uses 5G NR polar codes, 4-QAM and MaskGen over −5 to 5 dB ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05781)). STI-SemCom (31 Oct 2026) fuses an Emu3 autoregressive prior with LDPC belief propagation, but "at −0.2 dB and below more than 40% of tokens stay wrong" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00697)). Only two diffusion generators condition on digital content. PDSC (GLOBECOM 2026) conditions an SDXL-based latent diffusion model on 210 bits of quantised LaVIT and BLIP tokens, sent with unspecified "conventional channel coding and modulation" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14260)). The adaptive-request scheme (2510.26442) serialises frozen SD-VAE latents as IEEE-754 floats, applies a rate-1/2 convolutional code and maps to 16-QAM. A frozen Stable Diffusion inpaints the withheld blocks, and the receiver "has an accurate SNR estimate" ([arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)). These works are prior art for "diffusion receiver plus QAM link" only as separation. None of them learns a mapping to the constellation end to end.

**QAM-native generative JSCC without diffusion.** SemQAM (Cao, Ying, Huang, Qin, Shen; Tsinghua; 21 Oct 2026) is the nearest neighbour. Its codebooks hold "1024 codewords whose indices are mapped one to one to Gray-labeled 1024-QAM symbols", with no channel code. Generativity comes from LPIPS and "the generator-side PatchGAN loss following VQGAN". It reports LPIPS, DISTS and FID but **not DINO or PSNR**, and it does not compare against DiT-JSCC, DiffCom or DiffJSCC ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)). MOC-RVQ (GLOBECOM 2024) is an older relative. It aligns octonary codebooks with 64-QAM, notes that "we do not apply any channel error correction code", and restores features with a Swin noise-reduction block rather than diffusion ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01272)).

**Quantised digital JSCC that is not generative.** DeepJSCC-Q forms each symbol as "the softmax weighted sum of the symbols" of a fixed constellation with σq = 100. It tests QPSK through 64-QAM and up to 4096-QAM ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.13042)). MDJCM approximates modulation "by using scaled uniform noise" and then fine-tunes with STE across 4- to 1024-QAM in one model. It argues that soft-to-hard annealing suffers "biased or unstable gradients" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05437v2)). JCM samples constellation points with Gumbel-Softmax, and uJSCC switches modulation order by SNR boundaries; both are abstract or snippet level only. No paper found argues that digital modulation is incompatible with generative receivers. SemQAM concedes only that the finite constellation "can lower the achievable performance relative to continuous JSCC" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)).

| Family | Representative works | How symbols become digital | What the generative model does | SNR handling | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symbol-cleaning diffusion | SCDM, SSCDM, CD3M, DCDDM | JCM/VQ index mapped to 16- or 64-QAM | Denoises or corrects symbols; a deterministic decoder follows | Start step set from SNR (told) | SCDM/SSCDM full text; CD3M/DCDDM second-hand |
| Separation plus generative prior | TokCom, TG-TokCom, STI-SemCom, PDSC, 2510.26442 | Bits, then FEC with CRC, then QAM | Fills masked tokens, or diffusion conditioned on decoded content | MCS choice; SNR estimate; σ² in LLRs | Full text (PDSC code unspecified) |

## No generative receiver has been run blind against a specialist envelope

The SNR axis is where the repo's three-way study has the most room, and also where one reviewer comparison is predictable. SNR-told single models are already common: DiffJSCC, LRISC and OSD-JSCC take the true SNR, and the DiffCom family and the dual-domain posterior sampler use σ_n² in the likelihood ([DiffJSCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17736), [OSD-JSCC](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06437), [ADDPS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.16796.md)). DiffJSCC's only SNR ablation is presence versus absence of the condition: "augmenting the model with channel SNR information yields an average FID score improvement of 2%". It has no mismatch study ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17736)). Digital schemes are told as well. SemQAM selects "the corresponding VQ codebooks for each operating point" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)). SSCDM picks its start step by "k* = argmin |η − P/σ̄²|" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22983v1)). In the CRC-masking token schemes, the generator is SNR-agnostic by construction, because it sees only erasures.

**SGD-JSCC is the comparison reviewers will raise.** It claims "the performance of the proposed SGD-JSCC method remains nearly identical, whether the eq. SNR is available or not" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01138v1)). It achieves this by "directly estimating it from the noisy JSCC features" with a ResNet (4 residual blocks, an FC layer and a sigmoid) trained with MSE on artificially noised latents. The estimate is then mapped to a diffusion start step. It is analog, it refines a DeepJSCC output trained at a fixed 10 dB rather than generating from pure noise, and it reports no estimator accuracy and no estimated-versus-true ablation. That makes it estimate-then-tell, not blind. A blind DiT, though, can be argued to *implicitly* learn the same estimator. The prototype has to settle that argument with data, not rhetoric.

SC-CDM is a quieter risk to any "first blind diffusion JSCC" phrasing. "a single model covers a range of SNR from 0 dB to 15 dB", trained "with SNRs between 1 dB and 13 dB", and the paper describes no SNR input ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02121)). It is probably SNR-agnostic. It is also analog, restoration-type and CIFAR-scale, and it makes no told-versus-blind comparison.

The specialist envelope is missing everywhere. LRISC criticises methods that "typically train separate models for discrete SNR levels", but compares only against DeepJSCC and BPG+LDPC ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21577)). DiT-JSCC trains only specialists, and OSD-JSCC trains only a shared model. In the digital literature, only DeepJSCC-Q runs a mismatch study, in its Fig. 3. It attributes weak low-SNR 16-QAM results to "the SNREst used for the 16-QAM model being much greater than the channel SNR in that region" ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.13042)). So two things are unpublished in any form: a measured gap between one generative model and per-SNR specialists at each SNR, and a blind arm retrained without SNR input.

## Claims to drop, and the scoped claims that survive

Each tempting headline below already has a verified counterexample:

| Claim to avoid | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "First diffusion-based digital semantic communication" | SCDM, SSCDM, CD3M, DCDDM ([SSCDM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22983)); PDSC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14260)) |
| "First QAM-native / digital generative image JSCC" | SemQAM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29643v1)); MOC-RVQ ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.01272)) |
| "First diffusion receiver to generate images from digitally transmitted information" | PDSC; 2510.26442 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26442v4)); latency-aware SD SemCom ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17256)) |
| "First quantised JSCC trained once across SNRs" | DeepJSCC-Q 2021 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.13042)); MDJCM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05437v2)) |
| "First single-model, SNR-adaptive diffusion JSCC" | OSD-JSCC, LRISC, DiffJSCC, SC-CDM |
| "First diffusion JSCC that works without the true SNR" | SGD-JSCC (estimated); SC-CDM (likely agnostic) |
| "Digital generative transmission beats analog generative JSCC" | Contradicts DiT-JSCC's own separation results; would need a new matched-CBR study ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03112)) |

Four claims appear defensible, each with a stated residual risk.

The **architectural claim** is the strongest. It reads: "To our knowledge, the first learned JSCC whose channel input is constrained to a fixed standard M-QAM constellation and whose receiver is a conditional diffusion transformer generating the image from pure noise, conditioned only on the received constellation symbols." The residual risks are CD3M and DCDDM, whose full texts are unread. There may also be IEEE-only ICC or GLOBECOM 2026 papers, and the Tsinghua group (Zhijin Qin) has released SemQAM, the learned-constellation DLM-SC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30988)) and the flow receiver RC-BFM within six months. That group is the most likely source of a "SemQAM plus diffusion" paper.

The **empirical SNR claim** reads: "The first controlled comparison, for a generative diffusion JSCC receiver, of per-SNR specialists, one SNR-told model and one blind model, plus an estimated-SNR model, with the specialist envelope reported at each SNR." Its risk is narrower: PODD and JSCGC have been read only as abstracts, and the citation indexes are incomplete. It holds for the analog prototype alone, so it does not depend on the digital extension.

The **interaction claim** reads: "The first measurement of how 16-QAM quantisation changes the perceptual and semantic quality (LPIPS, FID, DINO) of a diffusion receiver relative to analog, at matched bandwidth, across SNR." SemQAM measures perceptual quality at 1024-QAM, but with a GAN decoder and without DINO. No digital JSCC paper found reports DINO metrics at all.

The narrowest claim is **a quantised DINOv2 semantic branch over a digital link**. PDSC quantises LaVIT, not DINOv2, tokens, and no DINOv2-to-bits or DINOv2-to-QAM work turned up ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14260)).

One caveat applies to all four. The prototype is a small DiT on the repo's p128 configuration, while DiT-JSCC uses ImageNet at 256 or 512 pixels. The claims should be framed as a controlled study, not a state-of-the-art result.

## The repo's own positioning needs four corrections

The most important correction concerns DeepJSCC-Q. The README quotes the DeepJSCC-Q future work: "Models are trained per SNR_train. Composing the ADJSCC AF module with the soft-to-hard quantiser is the obvious next step and is not done here" ([README.md](../README.md)). The comparison table in `docs/background/dit-jscc-explained.md` §10 accordingly marks DeepJSCC-Q "no" for one model for all SNRs ([dit-jscc-explained.md](../docs/background/dit-jscc-explained.md)). The 2021 conference version contradicts both. It explicitly contrasts itself with the "need to train multiple networks, one for each channel condition", trains on SNR ~ U[0,10] dB with an attention module fed the SNR, and runs a mismatch study ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.13042)). The quoted limitation may come from the JSAIT journal version, whose PDF could not be parsed, so the attribution should name the version or be removed. In either case, "soft-to-hard QAM quantiser plus SNR attention in one model" has direct prior art in DeepJSCC-Q's own lineage.

The README's fallback distinction is also weaker than stated. It says the composed works "all use *learned* VQ codebooks". MDJCM, however, quantises onto standard 4- to 1024-QAM and uses an SNR conditional module ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05437v2)). MDJCM has an internal inconsistency (Sec. V-A1 says "we train the model at an SNR of 10 dB"), which slightly softens that rebuttal, but it does not remove it. ADJSCC-Q should be presented as a reproduction and ablation of known components. Its contribution is the 2×2 evidence, not the architecture.

The other two corrections concern DiT-JSCC. The explainer says DiT-JSCC "reports no decoding time and does not state the number of sampling steps". v2 states, "All listed methods use 50 diffusion steps", and discusses encoding and decoding time against model size ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03112v2)). The explainer's "the code is public" also needs qualifying: the repository is inference-only, and "No model weights are included in this repository" ([GitHub](https://github.com/semcomm/DiTJSCC)).

## Five checks and seven experiments before claiming novelty

Five checks remain on the literature side, and none of them could be automated in this pass. First, run Google Scholar "cited by" for DiT-JSCC, SemQAM, SGD-JSCC and DeepJSCC-Q: Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 repeatedly, and Scholar was never queried. Second, read the full texts of CD3M (IEEE WCL 2026), DCDDM (IEEE TWC 2025), PODD (IEEE TMC 2026) and JSCGC, which conditions a generative receiver on the received signal "across channel conditions" with modulation unstated ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12858)). Third, get the DeepJSCC-Q JSAIT full text to settle what the journal version says about SNR adaptivity. Fourth, search the exact phrases the notes did not cover ("DeepJSCC-Q diffusion", "finite constellation diffusion JSCC", "modulation-aware generative JSCC", "digital DiT decoder") and check the ICC and GLOBECOM 2026 proceedings on IEEE Xplore directly, since IEEE-only papers are under-covered. Fifth, set arXiv alerts on the Qin (Tsinghua), Dai (BUPT) and Tang/Yang (Zhejiang) groups, and re-verify every quote against the PDFs before submission: the fetch tool that produced the notes summarises, so its quotes are near-verbatim, not exact.

Separate from these checks, the citation map for the paper itself is settled. The paper should cite and distinguish DiT-JSCC (analog, specialists), SGD-JSCC (estimator), OSD-JSCC (told, one-step), DiffJSCC (told), SC-CDM (agnostic restoration), SemQAM (QAM-native GAN), SSCDM and SCDM (symbol-cleaning diffusion), PDSC and 2510.26442 (separated diffusion receivers), and DeepJSCC-Q and MDJCM (quantisers).

The experiments matter more than the prose, because they turn a combination into a finding. The repo already has most of the machinery. `semcom/gen/config.py` defines `snr_mode` as "fixed", "snr" or "blank". "blank" is retrained with a constant SNR slot, which avoids the ambiguity left open in SGD-JSCC's ADJSCC baseline, where the notes could not tell whether the no-SNR arm was retrained or only had its input zeroed. `semcom/snr_estimation.py` already implements classical estimators, including M2M4 and decision-directed estimators ([snr_estimation.py](../semcom/snr_estimation.py)). Neither the gen model nor its config yet contains a QAM path.

| Experiment | What it settles | Pre-empts |
|---|---|---|
| {analog, 16-QAM} × {specialist, told, blind} at matched channel uses, −5 to 5 dB | Whether quantisation changes the value of SNR knowledge for a generative receiver | "Just DiT-JSCC plus DeepJSCC-Q" |
| **Estimated-SNR arm**: the told model fed (a) a classical estimator from `snr_estimation.py` and (b) an SGD-JSCC-style ResNet estimator. Report estimator RMSE per SNR | Whether blind ≈ estimate-then-tell; whether the blind DiT is an implicit estimator | The SGD-JSCC comparison |
| Mismatch curves: the told model given the true SNR ± 2–3 dB | How sensitive the generator is to mis-telling, against DeepJSCC-Q Fig. 3 | "Just use the true SNR" |
| Quantiser ablation: soft-to-hard (σq = 100) vs uniform noise + STE | Whether results hinge on the quantiser MDJCM criticises | Gradient-bias critique |
| Constellation order: 4-, 16- and 64-QAM | Where the digital–analog gap opens | "16-QAM is cherry-picked" |
| Conditioning ablation: raw received symbols vs hard-detected points vs soft demapper posteriors | Whether a DiT benefits from soft constellation information, which no diffusion receiver found uses | Novelty of the interface |
| Specialist envelope with confidence intervals on LPIPS, DINO and FID, on more than DiT-JSCC's 100 test images; cheap PAPR for analog vs QAM | Whether one model really closes the specialist gap | Thin evaluation |

The estimator arm has one more reason to exist. Quantisation makes blind SNR estimation easier. The repo's estimator module records that 16-QAM has kurtosis 1.32 against 2 for a complex Gaussian. It also notes that M2M4 "cannot separate a Gaussian-like signal from Gaussian noise" ([snr_estimation.py](../semcom/snr_estimation.py)). A blind digital DiT therefore has a cleaner implicit SNR cue than the analog one. If the blind arm matches the told arm only in the 16-QAM setting, that is a real, publishable interaction. It is also exactly the result a reviewer would otherwise dismiss as SGD-JSCC's estimator in disguise.

The SNR range adds context to that result; this comes from a capacity calculation, not from the sources. Gaussian capacity at −5 to 5 dB is about 0.4 to 2.1 bits per complex symbol, well below 16-QAM's 4 bits. So any digital penalty in this range should come from the quantiser's representation error rather than from the constellation capping the rate.

## Conclusion

The novelty question has moved from "has anyone done digital diffusion JSCC" to "which job does diffusion do, and what does the receiver know about the channel". On the first question, the field has split into three camps: analog generators, digital symbol cleaners, and digital separated generators. A DiT that generates from pure noise conditioned on learned QAM symbols falls in the gap between them, but the gap is narrow and the neighbours are weeks old. The SNR axis is more durable, because the specialist envelope and the blind-versus-estimated distinction are absent across all three camps, analog included. That suggests the strongest paper puts the three-way SNR study first and treats 16-QAM as the condition under which blindness might become cheap, not as the headline.

There is a practical consequence. The analog three-way study is publishable on its own and is lower-risk than the digital claim. Running the estimator arm and the specialist envelope now protects both papers. Adding the 16-QAM factor afterwards turns a gap that could be scooped into a measured interaction that is much harder to scoop.
